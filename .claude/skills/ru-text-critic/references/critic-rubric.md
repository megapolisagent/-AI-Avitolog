# Critic Rubric

Use this rubric to detect AI-slop and to push prose toward a more human, literary result. Apply it to any target language, adapting the concrete marker words to that language. For Russian, the examples below are especially important.

## Core Principle

Good prose is not just "clean." It has pressure, voice, rhythm, selection, and the courage to leave some things unsaid. The Critic should remove algorithmic smoothness without flattening the author's intent.

## What To Reward

- Meaning over word-preservation: syntax should feel native to the target language.
- Specificity: concrete nouns, actions, sensory anchors, exact social or emotional stakes.
- Rhythm: short sentences where impact is needed, longer sentences where thought naturally gathers.
- Productive imperfection: a live aside, abrupt turn, or asymmetry can be better than polished symmetry.
- Voice: diction should fit the speaker, audience, genre, and cultural context.
- Restraint: emotional force should usually come from the scene, fact, or contrast, not from adjectives announcing importance.

## AI-Slop Patterns To Flag

### Meaning Patterns

- Inflated significance: "plays a key role", "serves as a testament", "epoch-making", "crucial moment", "constantly evolving landscape".
- Empty media/social proof: "widely covered", "experts note", "observers say" without named evidence or relevance.
- Fake depth through participial tails: phrases equivalent to "reflecting", "symbolizing", "highlighting", "contributing to" when they merely explain the obvious.
- Advertising awe: "stunning", "rich", "picturesque", "nestled in the heart of", "unique atmosphere" without concrete support.
- Template sections: "Challenges and prospects", "Despite these challenges", "Looking ahead", or an obligatory optimistic final paragraph.

### Language Patterns

- AI marker words and transitions: "moreover", "furthermore", "however", "it is important to note", "let's delve", "seamless", "unprecedented", "dynamic", "unlock", "elevate".
- Russian marker words and calques: "Кроме того", "однако" as a mechanical transition, "важно отметить", "давайте углубимся", "бесшовный", "беспрецедентный", "динамичный", "подчеркивая".
- Bureaucratic bindings: "acts as", "serves as", "represents", "can boast" where a direct verb, dash, or simpler clause would work.
- Negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y".
- Rule of three: polished triads that sound generated, especially "improves X, optimizes Y, and increases Z".
- Forced synonym cycling: replacing a normal repeated word with a stilted synonym just to avoid repetition.
- False ranges: "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on the same measurable scale.

### Style Patterns

- Decorative dashes used to fake energy.
- Random bold emphasis inside prose.
- Bullet lists where every item starts with a bold mini-heading unless the format truly needs it.
- English Title Case in non-English headings.
- Inappropriate emoji or assistant-chat artifacts.
- Unnecessary quotation marks around metaphors, business slang, or ordinary figurative use. In Russian, keep «...» for real quotations, direct speech, titles, and product/company names; remove decorative quotes.

### Assistant Artifacts

- "Here is your text", "Of course", "Hope this helps", apologetic prefaces, knowledge disclaimers, or praise for the user's request.
- Hedge clutter: "ultimately", "largely", "potentially may", "one could argue", "at this stage", "in order to", and equivalent phrases in the target language.
- Empty upbeat endings: "the future looks bright", "exciting times ahead", "a step in the right direction".

## Literary Diagnosis Checklist

Ask:

- Does this review unit say something new or perform a necessary structural job, or does it only rephrase the brief?
- Could the same unit fit a dozen unrelated topics after swapping nouns?
- Are there images, verbs, and social details that belong only to this source material?
- Does the tone match the format, or does everything sound like a neutral essay?
- Is the unit too balanced, symmetrical, and frictionless for its form and intended job?
- Are there unsupported claims added for beauty or drama?
- Is the ending earned, or just a neat generated landing?

## Critic Output Standard

Be specific. Quote only the short fragment needed to identify the issue. Name the pattern and explain how the Literator should fix it without writing the replacement text.
